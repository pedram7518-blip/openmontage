# Is There a Loneliness Epidemic? — Script & Recording Guide

**Runtime:** ~10 minutes (≈1,500 spoken words at a normal commentary pace)
**Format:** You on camera (A-roll) + the social posts you've seen (clips) + stat cards (provided in `graphics/`) + optional B-roll
**Tone:** Empathetic but honest. You're the friend who takes it seriously *and* can still crack a joke. Never laugh **at** the people in the clips.

---

| Marker | Meaning |

| **YOU:** | Words to say on camera. Paraphrase freely — it should sound like you, not like reading. |

| `(direction)` | How to deliver the line / what to do physically. |

## BEFORE YOU RECORD — setup checklist

**Camera & framing**
- Shoot **16:9, 4K or 1080p, 30fps** (match whatever your previous videos use).
- Frame **chest-up, eyes on the top third line**, slightly off-center so you have room on the other side for clips/stat cards to pop in.
- Record **two framings** if you can: a normal medium shot and a **tight close-up** (or shoot in 4K and punch in during the edit). Cutting between them on serious lines is what makes commentary feel produced.
- Look **into the lens**, not at the screen.

**Light & sound**
- Face a window or put your main light ~45° to the side, slightly above eye level. Darker, moodier background suits this topic — one lamp in the back is enough.
- Mic close to you (lav or a shotgun just out of frame). Turn off fans/AC. Audio matters more than video.

**Performance**
- Record **section by section**. Do each section 2–3 times, then move on. It's normal.
- Clap once on camera at the start of each take — makes syncing easy.
- If you flub, pause, and **restart the whole sentence** — don't patch mid-sentence.
- The cold open and the "What actually helps" section are the most important. Do extra takes of those.

**Things to capture on your phone/computer (the clips)**
- Screen-record **5–8 of the posts** you've seen ("I'm __ and I've never had…"). Grab 5–10 seconds of each.
- Screen-record **the comment sections** under 2–3 of them — scroll slowly. The "same" comments are a big part of the story.
- **Blur usernames, profile pictures and faces of private people** in your edit. It's kinder, it protects you from harassment/privacy claims, and it makes the video more about the *trend* than about mocking any one person.
- Keep each clip short and talk over/after it. Short clips + your commentary = much stronger fair-use position than playing whole videos.

### 1. THE TREND (0:40 – 2:30)

**YOU:**
"So if you haven't seen these yet, the format is really simple. Someone looks at the camera — or just puts text on the screen — and says their age, and the thing they've never had. Never had a friend. Never been on a date. Never had someone to call on their birthday.

Some of them are joking. Some of them are crying. Most of them are somewhere in between. Let's look at a few."

`[CLIP 1]` — *play 5–10 sec, then pause it on screen*

**YOU:** (react genuinely — this is where your personality goes. Prompts below.)
- What's your honest first reaction?
- What detail stood out? (the room they're in, how calm they are, that they're laughing about it)
- "What gets me about this one is…"

`[CLIP 2]` → react. `[CLIP 3]` → react.

*Tip: keep each reaction 15–30 seconds. Don't over-explain. One sharp observation per clip beats three okay ones.*

**YOU:**
"But honestly? The videos aren't even the part that got me. It's the comments."

`[CLIP: screen recording scrolling a comment section — blur names]`

"'I'm 34. Same.' 'I thought I was the only one.' 'Didn't know other people felt this.' Thousands of them.

And look — I know the first instinct for a lot of people watching this is to go, *'Bro, just go outside.'* (half-smile) I get it. I've had that thought.

But when this many people are saying the same thing, at the same time, from different countries, different backgrounds… that's not a personal problem anymore. That's a pattern. So let's check if it's real."

### 2. THE NUMBERS (2:30 – 4:45)

**YOU:**
"Okay. Is this an actual epidemic, or just the algorithm feeding me sad videos because I watched two of them?

Let's start big. In 2025, the World Health Organization put out a whole report on loneliness. Their number:"

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/01_who_one_in_six.mp4]`

"**One in six people on Earth** report feeling lonely. Not 'a bit bored on a Friday.' Lonely.

And here's the part I didn't expect. You'd think the loneliest people would be, like, elderly people in nursing homes. Nope."

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/02_young_people.mp4]`

"The age group reporting the *highest* loneliness was **13 to 29 year olds**. The most connected generation in human history — with a phone that can reach anyone on the planet — is the loneliest. (⏸) That's insane to me.

And this isn't just a 'feelings' thing. The same report estimated loneliness is linked to about…"

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/03_deaths.mp4]`

"…**871,000 deaths a year**. That's roughly **100 people every hour**.

In the US, the Surgeon General actually declared loneliness a public health epidemic back in 2023 — and said the health risk is comparable to…"

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/04_cigarettes.mp4]`

"…**smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day**. (beat) So the guy who 'just needs to go outside' is basically being told that while his body's taking the hit of a pack-a-day habit.

Now — friendships specifically. There's a survey from 2021 that asked American men how many close friends they have."

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/05_no_close_friends.mp4]`

"In 1990, about **3%** of men said they had *zero* close friends. By 2021? **15%.** That's five times more. One in seven guys — no one.

And dating. You've probably seen this one floating around:"

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/06_single_men.mp4]`

"Pew Research found **63% of men under 30** describe themselves as single, compared to **34% of women** the same age.

Now — I'm gonna be fair here, because this stat gets thrown around a lot. Other researchers have pushed back and said that gap is probably smaller than it looks. But even the people arguing against it still found a real gap. So it's not made up — it's just not *quite* as dramatic as the headlines.

So, is it real? (⏸) Yeah. The data says yes. The people in these videos aren't outliers. They're just the ones saying it out loud."

### 3. WHY IS THIS HAPPENING? (4:45 – 7:15)

**YOU:**
"So the obvious question: *why?* Nobody has one perfect answer, but here are the five reasons that I think explain most of it. And stick around for number five, because I think it's the one nobody talks about."

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/07_reason_1.mp4]`  **1 — There's nowhere to go.**
"Think about where people used to make friends without even trying. The mall. The arcade. Church. Bowling leagues. The corner store you went to every day. Sociologists call these 'third places' — not home, not work, just somewhere you show up and see the same faces.

Now name one for you. (⏸) Where do you go, as an adult, to meet people — that isn't a bar or an app? For a lot of people, the honest answer is: nowhere."

`[B-ROLL: empty mall / empty parking lot / closed storefront]`

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/08_reason_2.mp4]`  **2 — Being alone got really, really comfortable.**
"Boredom used to be a feature. You got bored enough, you'd call somebody. Now? Infinite entertainment. Free. In your pocket. Forever. You can go a whole weekend and never feel bored enough to reach out. (beat) That's not you being weak. That's the most well-funded industry on the planet doing its job."

`[B-ROLL: phone screen glowing in a dark room / someone scrolling in bed]`

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/09_reason_3.mp4]`  **3 — Dating turned into a numbers game.**
"Meeting someone used to happen through people — friends of friends, school, work, a party. Now for a lot of people it's swiping. And apps reward whoever photographs well, not whoever's actually a good partner. If you're not getting matches, it's really easy to conclude you're the problem — and stop trying."

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/10_reason_4.mp4]`  **4 — Remote everything.**
"Remote work, online school, online gaming, groceries delivered. All great, honestly. But add it up and you can go an entire week where the only person you talk to face-to-face is the cashier. And a lot of us just… did. For years."

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/11_reason_5.mp4]`  **5 — The shame spiral.** (lean in — this is the important one)
"This is the one I think nobody talks about. The longer you go without friends, the *harder* it gets to start. Social skills are like a muscle — you don't use it, it gets weaker. So you get more awkward, so it goes worse, so you try less, so you get *more* awkward.

And then on top of that, there's shame. You're 28 and you feel like everyone else figured out something you missed. So you hide it. Which makes it worse.

(⏸) Which is why I actually think these videos — as sad as they are — might be a *good* sign. But let me explain."

### 4. THE COUNTERPOINT (7:15 – 8:15)

**YOU:**
"Here's the devil's advocate take, and I think it's fair: maybe people aren't *more* lonely. Maybe they're just *more willing to post about it*.

Twenty years ago, if you were 30 and had no friends, where would you even say that? You'd just… carry it. Now there's an app where you can say it to a million strangers — and an algorithm that pushes it to everyone who relates.

So some of what we're seeing is visibility, not just volume.

But — the surveys I showed you earlier weren't TikTok videos. They were phone and online surveys of thousands of random people, going back decades. And the trend is still there.

So I think the honest answer is: **both are true.** It's genuinely getting worse — *and* we're finally seeing it.

And honestly? Someone posting 'I'm 29 and I've never had a friend' is doing the bravest version of the thing they're struggling with. They're reaching out. Just to everyone at once."

---

### 5. WHAT ACTUALLY HELPS (8:15 – 9:30)

**YOU:** (shift energy — warmer, a bit more upbeat. This is the value section.)
"So if you're watching this and you're *in* one of these videos — or you could be — here's what actually helps. Not 'just be confident.' Actual stuff.

There's a study from the University of Kansas that tried to measure how long it takes to make a friend. Their estimate:"

`[GRAPHIC: graphics/video/12_hours_to_friend.mp4]`

"About **50 hours** together to go from acquaintance to casual friend. Around **90 hours** to become actual friends. And **200-plus hours** to become close friends.

(beat) Which means if you've been waiting to meet someone and instantly click — that's not how it works for *anybody*. Friendship is basically a time investment. So:

**One — pick a repeat place.** Same gym class, same time. A rec league. A climbing gym. A volunteer shift. A weekly game night at a card shop. The magic isn't the activity — it's seeing the *same people again and again* until those hours stack up.

**Two — be the inviter.** Almost everyone is waiting to be invited. If you're the one who says 'hey, a few of us are getting food after, you in?' — you become the hub. Feels terrifying the first time. Gets easier every time.

**Three — lower the bar.** You don't need a best friend this month. You need one person you say hi to by name. That's the first step on the 50 hours.

**Four — online friends count**, but try moving one of them one step closer. Text to voice. Voice to meeting up, if it's safe.

And if this has gone past lonely — if it's started to feel hopeless — please talk to someone. A doctor, a therapist, or if you're in the US you can call or text **988** any time. That's not weakness. That's the same thing as everything else in this video: reaching out.

And for everyone else — the ones who'd comment 'just go outside' — (⏸) next time you see one of these videos, just reply to it. Invite the quiet guy at work to lunch. You have no idea how many hours you're giving someone."

If you got something out of this, subscribe — I break down stuff like this from my feed every week. And I'll see you in the next one."

## EDITING TIPS (for when you cut it yourself)

1. **Cut every pause that isn't marked ⏸.** Keep the ⏸ ones. That's the rhythm.
2. **Punch-in (zoom 110–120%) on emphasis lines** — the stats, "That's insane to me," number five, the 988 line.
3. **Something should change on screen every 5–8 seconds** in the first 2 minutes (cut, zoom, clip, graphic). Slow down after the 4-minute mark once people are hooked.
4. **Music:** soft lo-fi or ambient piano under everything at about −25 dB below your voice; drop it to silence for the cold-open line *"Thirty-two. Never been invited to anything."* and for the 988 line. Free music with no account needed: YouTube Audio Library (in YouTube Studio), pixabay.com/music (search "sad lofi" or "ambient piano").
5. **Subtitles:** upload your final video to YouTube and let it auto-caption, then fix errors. Or send me the recorded audio and I can generate an `.srt` file for you locally.
6. **Blur** names, profile pics and faces of private people in the clips.
