# Style Playbook — reverse-engineered from the reference video

Reference: 25:02 commentary video ("checking in on the alphas"), 1920x1080 30fps.
Measured: 757 hard cuts (median shot 1.4s, ~28-35 cuts/min, faster in the back half),
~177 words/min delivery, 41 deliberate silences >1.5s (almost all right after a punchline or on a visual gag).

## 1. Format & structure
| Block | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:00-0:38 | Name gag ("Chad Chad. Alpha Chad Chad to you"), one-line premise, a relatable analogy (cow in the city vs the country), "I found two that I *have* to show you" |
| Subject 1 intro | 0:38-1:00 | Subject's own clip first, then a big serif NAME CARD over his face, then a deadpan list-description ("biohacking, crypto-trading, e-commerce-ing ... millionaire") |
| Clip → react loops | 1:00-4:24 | Play 3-15s of subject clip → cut to her → 1-3 jokes → next clip |
| Sponsor | 4:24-5:37 | Segued off the last joke ("have you ever heard of... today's sponsor?"); sponsor read is still a bit (flirting with the game's villain, costume change, QR code) |
| Escalation | 5:37-13:15 | Each mini-topic: claim → receipts (clip) → reframe → callback |
| Pivot to subject 2 | 13:15 | Bridge line + a montage of his clips narrated deadpan ("He can dive his face into water.") |
| Earnest turn | 22:00 | 30s of sincere point ("they have routines because they're rich, don't compare yourself") |
| Closing skit | 22:35-24:35 | She parodies BOTH subjects in a fully-shot routine (fake abs, timestamps, ice-water bowl), ending on a shrine/candle gag |
| Outro | 24:36-25:02 | Casual sign-off, "Okay, bye." + socials card |

## 2. How the jokes are built (the writing engine)
1. **Literal-minded misreading.** Take the subject's words literally and follow them to an absurd place.
   "Ask rich people for money... it's a side hustle. Because you're *hustling* them." / "Neglect celebrities" → "but we're supposed to be making rich friends."
2. **Deadpan understatement after a wild clip.** "Those things were very helpful." → beat of silence.
3. **Rule of three that turns on beat 3.** "The sun's not out... businesses aren't open... the worms aren't even awake, so he can't even get one of those either."
4. **Wordplay pivots, always as a throwaway.** "You're gonna be so Thor tomorrow." / "Fitness coach into the back of his car." / "The only taint in his mouth is Tristan."
5. **Math/fact-check as comedy.** She actually does the math (birth-control numbers, 9 hours of sleep), then undercuts herself: "Why am I doing so much math in this video?"
6. **Callbacks.** "I think people need to start studying words" gets replayed against him later; "Give me a $10 million contract" reappears as a transition; water, Tristan, ice and "the hands" thread the whole video.
7. **Escalating mini-skits.** A premise becomes a 10-40s acted scene with her playing every role (bro + bro, Dunder-Mifflin paper salesman, knight & squire, 70-person-orgy "it's 5am").
8. **Mock-sincere asides to the audience.** "If you watch reels for over an hour a day... don't do that. [beat] So do I."
9. **Fake-out sincerity → insult.** "He does seem like royalty... because he's a little princess."
10. **Meta lines.** "That's foreshadowing for later in the video. It'll make sense later."

## 3. Delivery
- Default register: dry, low-energy, slightly bored, fast (~177 wpm). She never sells a joke with her voice.
- **The pause is the punchline.** Hold 1-2s of dead air after the line, often with a look to camera, a slow blink, or a tiny head tilt.
- Big energy only in skits (character voices, accents, yelling) — contrast with the deadpan host.
- Physical bits: leaves frame and walks back, phone-call to the side, hand gestures toward clips, single eyebrow.

## 4. Editing language (what to replicate)
| Device | How it's used |
|---|---|
| Jump cuts | Every breath/pause cut out of talking sections except intentional comedy pauses |
| Punch-in zoom | Alternate wide ↔ ~130-160% crop every 1-3 lines; tight punch-in on the punchline |
| Vertical clips | 9:16 TikTok/Reels centered on a **blurred, zoomed copy of itself** as background; native captions & watermarks left in |
| Name cards | Big black serif (Times-style) name on white/over face: "LUKE BELMAR", "CHAD" |
| PIP shrink | Her facecam shrinks to a small box on black / over stock footage when she's "looking at" something |
| Hard cut to stock/meme | Literal visual for a word (a riverbank, money stacks, a toilet, a monkey drinking water, "CLOSED" sign) held 1-2s |
| Green-screen insert | Her keyed into stock scenes (office, knights, forest, pilot costume) |
| Red circle | Hand-drawn red ellipse on the detail she's mocking (timestamps) |
| Floating words | Repeated word scattered across a stock image ("that that that") |
| Dry on-screen labels | Small serif captions: "8x speed" |
| Black & white | Facecam goes B&W for fake-dramatic/sad beats |
| Censor bar & emoji | Black bar over mouth, sweat-drop emoji |
| Sound | No constant music bed; music/SFX only under skits and montages; silence sells the deadpan |

## 5. Look
- Single static facecam, eye level, medium shot, broadcast mic on boom arm in frame.
- Busy, personality-heavy background: posters, plant, practical lamp (warm red-orange key on wall) + cyan/blue orb light — warm/cool contrast.
- Natural color, no heavy grade, no burned-in subtitles on her own talking.

## New joke mechanics seen here
- **Follow the rule's logic until it backfires.** "Don't say where you're going" → "If you say 'pardon me, I'll be right back,' I'm like, okay, they're doing drugs in the bathroom."
- **Reframe what the rule is really for.** "How to exit a room... make everyone feel a little distrusted."
- **The laziest alternative.** "Or the most ladylike method: just leave it there. Cut your losses."
- **Opposite enactment.** "I slip into something more comfortable. High heels and a ball gown."
- **Find the double standard, then go absurd.** "There's no equivalent rule for men... water bottles? Phallic."
- **History fact, then a blunt closer.** Sidesaddle: "you might fall off... but at least you won't be a slut."
- **Cut off mid-sentence.** "If you need to take a massive, steaming, wet— [beat] No? Okay, let's eat."
- **Callbacks across sections.** "I thought we weren't supposed to talk about [the bathroom]."
- **A skit genre swerve.** A bathroom joke turns into a fantasy epic ("18 years ago, that place took my family... the realm is free").
- **A short sincere point** before the finale: "Isn't choosing a hobby based on what other people think of it defeating the purpose?"

## New editing devices
| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| She's keyed into the setting the line names | Chili's, a palace ballroom, an ambulance, an airplane, a Victorian sidesaddle illustration |
| Spinning keyed composite | She's inside the washing machine drum, rotating (sponsor gag) |
| Costume cutaways | Powdered wig and gown for "the elegant version" |
| Dream/fantasy glow | Purple haze and soft vignette for the imagined scenario |
| Tilted PIP on black | The cam shrinks and rotates as a reaction beat |
| ✅ / ❌ stickers | Pasted onto the creators' own "do/don't" clips |
| Sponsor timer | A small "chAD time" ring timer in the corner during the ad read, plus bold condensed white spec text |
| Hand over lens | Final transition into the end card |
| Handheld footage for the closing skit | Low angles, whip pans, a cut to her sneakers for the reveal |

# THE FORMULA (constant across both videos)
1. **Cold open:** a skit or gag that acts out the topic → "Hey guys, my name is Chad Chad [twist]" → a one-line premise.
2. **First joke within 25 seconds,** and an absurd analogy within the first minute.
3. **The core loop:** CLIP (their words) → HER (take it literally / follow the logic / reframe the motive) → PAUSE → optional SKIT where she plays every role → next clip. Each loop runs 20-60 seconds.
4. **The sponsor sits about 20-25% in,** segued off the previous joke, and the read is a bit itself with a running gag.
5. **Escalate:** the later rules or claims are more absurd, and the callbacks pile up.
6. **One short sincere beat** (15-30 seconds) at about 85-90%.
7. **A bridge line** ("I could do that too" / "it's like the Hunger Games but...") leads into the **closing parody skit,** shot on location in costume. This is the biggest production moment of the video.
8. **Fixed outro:** "I'm gonna call it there, guys. Thank you for subscribing, and if you are still here, thank you so much for watching... Okay, bye."
9. **Edit rules:** jump cuts on every gap except the comedy pauses. Punch-in on the punchline. Vertical clips on a blurred copy of themselves. A literal visual for any noun that can be pictured. Herself keyed into whatever place she names. No music under her talking.

# FORMULA v2 — use this going forward (supersedes the earlier formula)

## A. What's consistent across all three (non-negotiable)
| # | Rule | Ref 1 | Ref 2 | Ref 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold-open gag or skit **before** the greeting | name gag | "no, you first" | machete candy |
| 2 | "Hey guys... my name is Chad Chad" + a twist in the same sentence | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 3 | An umbrella thesis that **reframes** the topic | "checking in on the alphas" | "becoming ladylike" | "aging is the spookiest thing" |
| 4 | First joke by 0:25, an absurd analogy by 1:00 | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 5 | Sponsor at 2:30-4:30 (≈18-25%), segued off the joke before it | 4:24 | 2:45 | 2:35 |
| 6 | Clip → literal take → pause → skit loop | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 7 | 6-10 acted skits per video, she plays every role | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 8 | A short sincere beat at about 85% | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 9 | A closing parody skit shot on location, built from the video's callbacks | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 10 | Verbatim outro → "Okay, bye." → hand over lens → socials card | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

## B. Target numbers per 10 minutes of runtime
- 1,750-1,950 words of script (175-195 wpm when spoken).
- 20-35 cuts per minute. Never hold a static shot longer than about 6s unless it's a comedy pause.
- 1 joke every 10-15 seconds in host sections.
- 4-7 skits (10-40s each), and at least 3 callbacks.
- 15-40 deliberate pauses over 1.5s (about 2-4 per minute), each one right after a punchline.
- Clip share: 25-40% of runtime is source clips. Each clip runs 3-15s, then she cuts back.

## C. The segment beat sheet (repeat this 6-10 times)
1. **SETUP** (1 line): "Some of these videos involve really specific scenarios, like..."
2. **RECEIPT** (3-15s clip, vertical on a blurred copy, original captions left in)
3. **PUNCH** (1-2 lines). Pick one engine:
   literal reading · follow the logic to its extreme · reframe the motive · laziest alternative · opposite enactment · double standard · word-meaning attack · do the real math · fake formal or legal framing
4. **PAUSE** (1-2s, punch-in, deadpan look)
5. **TAG or SKIT** (optional): "That's like..." → a skit where she plays all the roles, ending on a callback or a cut-off line
6. **BRIDGE** into the next setup (no dead "next up")

## D. Script skeleton (fill in for any topic)
```
[COLD OPEN SKIT 5-15s]  act out the topic; end on a hard joke
"Hey guys, my name is ___ ___, [twist]."
[UMBRELLA THESIS]  reframe the topic in one sentence (+ seasonal hook if relevant)
[ABSURD ANALOGY]  "It's exactly like ___ ... [extend one step too far]"
[PERSONAL ANGLE]  "When I was growing up / until I downloaded TikTok..." → mini skit
[MOCK-HUMBLE SETUP]  "Turns out I've been doing everything wrong."
[SEGMENT x2]  beat sheet C
[SPONSOR SEGUE]  pun or pivot off the last joke → read (sincere or bit) → "let's get back to the video"
[SEGMENT x4-7]  escalating; each later one uses callbacks
[SINCERE BEAT 15-30s]  the real point, plainly said (can be delivered as a skit)
[BRIDGE TO FINALE]  "Even I could ___ if I combined everything we learned today."
[CLOSING SKIT 45-120s]  on location, costume, timestamps or a chant built entirely from callbacks; button on the earliest callback
[VERBATIM OUTRO]  → "Okay, bye." → hand over lens → socials
```

## E. Edit checklist (QC before export)
- [ ] Every breath and gap is cut, except the marked comedy pauses
- [ ] The punchline lands on a punch-in (130-160%), and the pause holds on it
- [ ] Every nameable noun or place has a cutaway or a green-screen composite of the host
- [ ] Vertical clips on a blurred, zoomed copy of themselves; original captions and watermark left in
- [ ] A name card or title card for each subject or series (big black serif / parchment)
- [ ] At least one annotation per segment: red circle, ✅/❌, censor bar, emoji, dry serif label ("8x speed")
- [ ] Facecam treatments for beats: B&W (fake drama), shrink to PIP on black or tilted (reaction), glow or haze (imagined)
- [ ] No music under host talking; music or SFX only under skits, montages and the finale
- [ ] Sponsor: a corner timer ring + bold spec text + a CTA card with the code
- [ ] A recurring visual prop that's bookended (e.g. the clipboard list)
- [ ] End card: hand-over-lens wipe → socials

## F. Quality bar ("is this as good as the references?")
1. Could the cold open run as a standalone short? If not, rewrite it.
2. Is there a joke in every 15-second window of host talk?
3. Does the finale reuse at least 3 earlier jokes? If not, plant more callbacks earlier.
4. Is the delivery dry everywhere except inside skits?
5. Does the video say one true thing sincerely, once?
